# Scene Density

Use this optional mode for whole-scene planning when additional states help decide staging, continuity or timing. It changes the panel plan, not delivery mode, approval or reference eligibility. Single-image grid remains the multi-panel default; use separate images when requested or needed for individual revisions.

## Choose the level

| Level | Coverage | Useful for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | One decisive panel per shot | Quiet dialogue, montage, product beauty, limited budgets |
| L2 | Key shots get start, peak and end; other shots get one panel | Narrative reveals, meaningful camera moves and state changes |
| L3 | Key shots get visible sub-beats such as anticipation, contact, consequence and reaction; other shots get one panel | Physical turns, action, chases and complex blocking |

Record the level and reason. L2 is the default within this optional mode. Add states only when they change a staging decision; a shot may depart from the level with a stated reason. Do not invent contact or reactions where none occurs.

A shot is key when it carries the scene's turn, a camera move that changes framing or reveals information, a blocking change, an axis crossing or restatement, a prop or wardrobe state change, or a light cue. A held shot with only a blocking or state change may need start and end rather than three redundant panels.

## Honor the budget

An explicit panel count wins over the level. Count the proposed panels before writing prompts. If the budget cannot cover every state, select decisive states, map every shot to a panel or combined beat, and record omitted transitions as motion notes. Never silently expand a one-panel request or count alternative candidates as narrative panels.

## Author the states

Copy a supplied shot plan's framing, featured subjects, camera and requested light into the panel plan. Describe one frozen moment per panel and keep camera travel in motion notes. Name anatomy and frame side explicitly. Reuse the same approved element references across a shot's states; track prop ownership, wardrobe, damage, eyelines and travel direction.

Separate panels carry no labels or borders. Grids follow the existing grid layout contract. Later video use follows the entrypoint's approved-panel and explicit supported-conditioning rules; density grants no approval.
